We leave for Vietnam this evening. Our 4th year in a row, made possible by Amplify. I've never had a job that allowed this kind of time off and the funds to do so. Wish we could have done this as a family, meaning taking all 3 kids at one point when they were younger. That still remains a dream. Having all 3 kids together in one place, celebrating each one of them for existing and making the best of what they have and where they are.

I learned yesterday that Nicolai quit his job 3 weeks ago. I'm actually surprised he hadn't done it sooner. He's weathered through all his bosses, stayed longer than anyone else. Well, yeah, that was his one and only job since graduating from UCSB. He actually started with them the summer before, between his junior and senior year. So proud of him for sticking with it. He's spending this time just exploring, much needed I'm sure.

Sabrina has made it back to Scotland. Her visa expired for South Korea. She did say she'll try to make it back to Seoul someday.

Gabriel got offered a job on the spot yesterday! Up in Summerland, so it's a bit of a drive, 30 miles, not bad, especially since he'll have to leave early anyway and avoid the potential traffic. He'll be a line cook, something he has a lot of experience with. So happy for him. Thank you, God! He got a Juiced bike also, best purchase. He's been taking it out every day. Clears that mind and gets that body moving. If I loved and worried about that boy any more, my heart would crack open.

We allowed plenty of time to get to the airport. Our Lyft driver was actually more talkative than Thomas. Hard to believe. We did have great conversations for two hours. Stephen, 63 years old, black guy. Black guy whose bucket list is to do standup comedy. Turns out he's good enough to hang out with the regulars. Raised by his mom who passed away 3 years ago, nothing but good memories from his childhood. Married to a Japanese lady, he's finally happy apparently. His previous marriage lasted 16 years, supposedly, and he refers to it as "sixteen years of slavery." Haha.

Thomas is managing his spinal surgery. I finally saw his x-ray post surgery. Yeah, there are braces and brackets inside his back, a replaced disk. Scary. I can't imagine. But I hear about his constant pain. I admire his fortitude. Is that the right word? I know you do what you gotta do, but I'm not sure I have it in me. One little pain in my body and I'm wimping out, wondering if normalcy will ever be restored. I think I'd rather die.

That was a long-ass walk to gate 208. Thomas is getting the wheelchair and cart ride treatment. I live at the airport for a living, so I'm used to the long walks from gate to gate. I thank God for every step that I can take, especially looking around and seeing how many people need wheelchair assistance, either from injuries like Thomas and/or from old age. It is all too much. Would I ever want to travel if I can't walk on my own?
